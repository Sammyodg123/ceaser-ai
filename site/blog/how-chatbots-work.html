How AI Chatbots Actually Work

Chatbots predict the next word, one piece at a time, using patterns learned from enormous amounts of text.

Modern AI chatbots are powered by large language models (LLMs). During training, the model reads vast amounts of text and learns which words and ideas tend to follow one another.

When you type a prompt, the text is split into small pieces called tokens. The model then predicts the most likely next token, adds it, and repeats until the answer is complete. That is why replies appear to stream word by word.

After the first training stage, models are refined with human feedback so answers become more helpful, polite and safe.

Because the model predicts plausible text rather than looking up verified facts, it can sometimes state something wrong with confidence. This is called hallucination, and it is why checking sources matters.
